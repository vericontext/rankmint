## Newsletter Body

**THE CITY RAMEN VALUE INDEX**
*30 cities. 5 metrics. One surprising winner.*

---

Here is what travel media gets wrong about ramen: it asks "where is the best ramen?" when the more interesting question is "where is ramen best *for you* — the person who lives there, eats there regularly, and doesn't treat it as a bucket-list event?"

That's the question behind the City Ramen Value Index. Five weighted dimensions:

- **Affordability (25%)** — how many bowls can you buy per month's wages?
- **Shop Density (20%)** — how many dedicated ramen restaurants per 100,000 residents?
- **Quality Ceiling (20%)** — how good is the best ramen actually available?
- **Style Variety (20%)** — how many of the 9 core styles can you access?
- **Cultural Depth (15%)** — is ramen a way of life here, or a trend from three years ago?

The results, scored 0-10 and normalized across all 30 cities:

---

**S-TIER: The Pilgrimage Cities**

**#1 Fukuoka — 9.21**
The birthplace of tonkotsu earns a perfect 10 on Cultural Depth. This is a city where the yatai stalls (outdoor food stalls) have been serving ramen since before most ramen critics were born, where the local style (Hakata-style thin noodles in milky pork bone broth) has an almost religious following, and where new shops open constantly because the demand never stops. Prices remain lower than Tokyo. The formula rewards all of this accordingly.

**#2 Tokyo — 8.91**
The only city to score a perfect 10 on Style Variety — all 9 styles are regularly available, often on the same block. More Michelin Bib Gourmand ramen recognitions than any city on Earth. The shop density score (10.0) is the index maximum. Tokyo is not being dethroned because it's bad. It's being dethroned because Fukuoka is better *on the terms that matter most for actual ramen culture.*

**#3 Osaka — 8.39**
Near-Tokyo density at slightly lower prices, with a distinct regional food identity that gives ramen a different character than you'll find two hours north on the Shinkansen.

**#5 Sapporo — 7.76**
The spiritual home of miso ramen. The "Ramen Yokocho" (Ramen Alley) is one of Japan's most-visited food streets. Cultural Depth score: 9.0 — the third-highest in the index.

---

**A-TIER: The Challengers**

**#4 Taipei — 8.15**
This is the number that will surprise you most. Taipei beats every non-Japanese city in the index — by a significant margin. Michelin-trained chefs (including alumni of the world's first Michelin-starred ramen restaurant) have built serious operations here. Eight distinct styles are available. Pricing sits near Bangkok levels — dramatically below what you'd pay in Hong Kong, Singapore, or anywhere in the West. Taipei is the most underrated ramen city in the world, and this index makes that concrete.

**#6 Seoul — 7.22**
Ramyeon culture in Korea runs so deep it developed its own vocabulary, its own instant noodle pantheon, and its own restaurant typology distinct from Japanese ramen. The embeddedness score (8.0) reflects a food culture where noodle soups are a daily staple, not a splurge.

**#7 Bangkok — 6.90**
Bangkok carries the highest Affordability score in the entire index: 9.41 out of 10. A quality ramen bowl here costs around $6 relative to local wages — meaning a local earns enough to eat significantly more bowls per month than their equivalent in New York or London. Japanese expat communities seeded the quality; Thai culinary creativity has begun producing genuine local fusion variants. Bangkok is a genuine ramen destination that Western food media has chronically underrated.

---

**THE TOURIST TAX**

**#17 New York — 4.47**
World-class quality ceiling (7.0) — Ivan Ramen, Ippudo, Tonchin are legitimately excellent. But New York's Affordability score is 0.00. That's the minimum in the entire index. A bowl costs $22 on average. The formula correctly identifies this as the worst value proposition among cities with a serious ramen scene.

**#27 London — 3.04**
£22 per bowl. 1.1 shops per 100,000 residents. A scene driven entirely by trend cycles rather than embedded food culture. Cultural Depth score: 3.0. London has ramen. London does not have a ramen scene.

**#30 Stockholm — 2.34**
Dead last. Scandinavian pricing applied to imported culture with near-zero shop density. The math is unforgiving and it should be.

**METHODOLOGY NOTE**

All component scores are normalized 0-10 before weighting. Affordability is calculated as (avg monthly wage) / (bowl price x 30), giving a "ramen purchasing power" figure that penalizes high-cost cities regardless of absolute wage levels. Quality Ceiling and Cultural Embeddedness are rubric-scored using anchors from Michelin Guide, Tabelog, Time Out, and The Infatuation. Full methodology in the [report](report.md).

Data collected March 2026. Sources: Numbeo (pricing, wages), worldpopulationreview.com (population), Michelin Guide, and city-specific shop density research.

# Global Nap Culture Index
> Spain invented the siesta. Japan perfected it. But the Netherlands — a country with no nap tradition whatsoever — nearly beat them both.

## Opening

There is a country where workers sleep an average of 5.87 hours a night — the least of any nation on Earth. Its offices run on caffeine, social pressure, and the quiet shame of being the first one to leave. Yet that same country has dedicated nap pods in train stations, a culturally revered concept of falling asleep on the job as a sign of dedication, and more rest infrastructure per capita than Spain. That country is Japan. And it ranks 6th on the Global Nap Culture Index — not because its people are well-rested, but because the system around them is engineered for opportunistic rest.

This index does not measure how much people actually sleep. It measures how optimized a country's culture, work structure, climate, and infrastructure are for the art of the nap. Those are different things. A nation can be sleep-deprived and nap-optimized simultaneously — Japan proves it. A nation can have a famous siesta tradition and still rank below Denmark, a country with no such history — Greece proves that too. The Global Nap Culture Index ranks 30 countries across five weighted dimensions: sleep duration, cultural permission, workload, climate, and infrastructure. The result is a map of where the world has truly solved the rest problem, versus where it has simply branded itself as doing so.

The biggest surprise in the data is not Japan. It is the Netherlands. A country where napping in public is no more culturally embedded than it is in Germany scores a near-perfect 10.0 on Work-Life Balance — the result of the world's shortest average workweek at 26.57 hours — and the highest infrastructure score after Japan. When you have that much structural space for rest, the tradition almost builds itself. The Netherlands finished 2nd overall, 0.11 points behind Spain, with a CultureScore of just 4 out of 10. It nearly beat the country that invented the siesta through sheer structural efficiency alone.

## The Surprise

**Surprise 1: The world's strongest nap culture produces one of the worst sleepers.**

Japan's CultureScore of 9 is tied with China for the highest in the index. Inemuri — the practice of "sleeping whilst present" — is a culturally specific form of napping that frames unconsciousness at one's desk not as laziness but as evidence of total dedication. Companies celebrate it. Commuters practice it openly on the Shinkansen. Japan has more nap pod installations in commercial buildings and transit hubs than any other country in the survey. And yet Japan's workers average just 5.87 hours of sleep per night — the absolute floor of the dataset, earning a SleepScore of 0.00 out of 10. The infrastructure exists because the deprivation demands it. Japan did not build a nap culture out of leisure. It built one out of necessity.

**Surprise 2: The Netherlands nearly beat Spain without trying.**

Spain ranks first with 7.07 points. The Netherlands ranks second with 6.96 — a gap of just 0.11 points. The Netherlands scores 4 out of 10 on CultureScore: there is no Dutch word for the afternoon nap, no tradition of midday rest, no cultural ceremony around sleep. What the Netherlands has instead is the shortest average workweek of any developed economy in the survey: 26.57 hours. A WorkloadScore of 10.0, perfectly normalized. Combined with an InfraScore of 8 driven by high park density and European cafe culture, the Netherlands has manufactured the conditions for napping without ever developing the intention. If the formula weighted work-life balance at 30% instead of 20%, the Netherlands would be #1.

**Surprise 3: Greece scores higher than Italy despite working significantly more hours.**

Conventional wisdom groups Spain, Italy, and Greece as equivalent Mediterranean siesta cultures. They are not. Greece averages 38.3 hours of work per week — 4.4 more than Italy's 33.9 — yielding a WorkloadScore of just 3.89 versus Italy's 6.16. Yet Greece (6.39) outscores Italy (6.30) because its CultureScore of 8 — backed by the kalo mesimeri tradition and legal protections for midday rest in residential areas — carries enough weight to compensate. Italy's siesta culture (riposo) has eroded more substantially in urban areas, earning a CultureScore of 7. One point of culture, weighted at 30%, can override nearly 2 points of structural disadvantage.

## Methodology

- **Index name**: Global Nap Culture Index (Nap Optimization Score, NOS)
- **Scoring formula**: `NOS = (SleepScore × 0.20) + (CultureScore × 0.30) + (WorkloadScore × 0.20) + (ClimateScore × 0.15) + (InfraScore × 0.15)`
- **Sample size**: 30 countries
- **Collection date**: March 2026

**Sub-score normalization:**
- SleepScore: `(country_hours − 5.87) / (7.47 − 5.87) × 10` — range: Japan (5.87h) to New Zealand (7.47h)
- WorkloadScore: `(45.69 − country_hours) / (45.69 − 26.57) × 10` — range: India (45.69h) to Netherlands (26.57h)
- ClimateScore: `max(0, 10 − |avg_temp − 25| × 0.4)` — optimized at 25°C
- CultureScore: 0–10 qualitative rubric assessing named nap traditions, legal protections, and cultural stigma
- InfraScore: 0–10 qualitative rubric assessing nap pods, park density, quiet-hour laws, and cafe lingering culture

**Data sources:**
- Average daily sleep duration: [worldpopulationreview.com — Average Sleep Time by Country](https://worldpopulationreview.com/country-rankings/average-sleep-time-by-country) (OECD time-use data)
- Average weekly working hours: [worldpopulationreview.com — Average Work Week by Country](https://worldpopulationreview.com/country-rankings/average-work-week-by-country) (ILO/OECD sourced)
- Average annual temperature: [fittotravel.net — Countries by Average Temperature](https://www.fittotravel.net/international-data/348-countries-by-average-temperature) (World Bank 1991–2016 climate normals)
- CultureScore and InfraScore: Qualitative rubric applied by RankMint; supplementary reference [Open Access Government — Customs from Countries](https://www.openaccessgovernment.org/customs-from-countries/59117/)

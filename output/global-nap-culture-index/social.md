## LinkedIn

**Full post:**

We ranked 30 countries by how structurally optimized they are for the art of the nap — and the results broke almost every assumption we had.

**Spain ranks first. But barely.**

The Netherlands ranked second — a country with no named nap tradition, no cultural ceremony around midday rest, and a CultureScore of just 4 out of 10. What the Netherlands has instead is the world's shortest average workweek (26.57 hours) and some of the most functional rest infrastructure in Europe. The gap between #1 Spain and #2 Netherlands: 0.11 points. The country that invented the siesta almost lost to a country that invented the bicycle.

**Japan ranked 6th.**

Not because Japanese workers are rested — they average just 5.87 hours of sleep per night, the absolute floor of our dataset. Japan ranked 6th because its rest infrastructure is extraordinary: nap pods in train stations and offices, the inemuri cultural norm that frames sleeping at your desk as a sign of dedication, and a near-perfect InfraScore of 9. Japan built a world-class nap ecosystem as a response to deprivation, not out of luxury.

**China scored 9/10 on culture and ranked 19th overall.**

Xiuxi (午休) is literally state-mandated rest time, written into Chinese labor law and cultural expectation across the country. Yet Chinese workers average 44.6 hours per week — a WorkloadScore of just 0.57. The state gives workers the right to nap. The workplace takes away the time.

**South Korea ranked last.**

Score: 2.98 out of 10. The country with arguably the world's strongest hustle culture scores 2 on nap culture tradition, 2 on infrastructure, and second-worst on sleep duration. Every metric points the same direction.

The methodology: five weighted metrics — sleep duration (20%), cultural permission (30%), workload (20%), climate (15%), and infrastructure (15%) — all normalized to a 0-10 scale across 30 countries.

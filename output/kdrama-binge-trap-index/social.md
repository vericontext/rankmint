### Post 1 — Main report post

I built a scoring model to find out which K-drama most reliably destroys your sleep schedule — and the results completely inverted the IMDb rankings.

The Binge Trap Score measures five structural addiction mechanisms: how dense the cliffhangers are, how long the romantic tension stretches, how ferociously spoilers circulate on social media, how many hours the show demands in total, and how frequently the plot detonates surprises.

Three findings that will frustrate K-drama fans:

1. Crash Landing on You (BTS 7.60) outscores Squid Game (BTS 7.40). The formula says unresolved romantic longing across a military border is a more persistent compulsion loop than survival fear. Squid Game resolves tension each episode — someone lives, someone dies. CLOY accumulates longing for 16 episodes without relief.

2. Penthouse — which critics spent years calling trashy — is the only drama in the index to score a perfect 10 on both Cliffhanger Density and Plot Twist Velocity simultaneously. No other drama achieves this. It is the most precisely constructed addiction machine in the dataset.

3. Reply 1988 (IMDb 9.0, the highest-rated K-drama on IMDb) ranks #29 out of 30 for binge danger. Its magic is its slowness: 20 episodes × 95 minutes = 31.7 total hours. It trusts you to come back. It scores low precisely because it is the opposite of a trap.

### Post 2 — The paradox angle

A data question I've been thinking about: is quality the same as addictiveness?

For K-dramas, the answer is definitively no.

I ranked 30 shows using a Binge Trap Score — five metrics that measure structural addiction architecture, not quality. The correlation with IMDb ratings is nearly zero.

The top-rated drama on IMDb (Reply 1988, 9.0) finishes #29 out of 30. The #1 binge trap (Crash Landing on You, BTS 7.60) is a romance — not the survival game that launched K-dramas globally.

What this suggests: the features that make a drama "good" — emotional pacing, character depth, thematic restraint — are largely orthogonal to the features that make it impossible to stop watching: hard cliffhangers, unresolved tension, and social spoiler pressure.

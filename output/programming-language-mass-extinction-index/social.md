**The Programming Language Mass Extinction Index — 30 languages ranked by forensic risk of dying.**

When Adobe killed Flash Player in December 2020, ActionScript died with it in weeks. Not gradually. Not gracefully. It ceased. That's a 100/100 extinction score — the only language in history where we can name the exact person who decided it would never recover.

But most language deaths aren't like that. They're geological. Slow. Almost imperceptible until they're undeniable.

We built a six-factor index — TIOBE presence, Stack Overflow activity, GitHub repo creation rate, job market demand, ecosystem health, and community vitality — to score 30 programming languages by extinction risk. Here's what the data actually shows:

**The surprises that matter:**

COBOL scores 78/100 and ranks #10 most endangered. That sounds alarming until you look at the job market: 1,400+ open positions, IBM apprenticeship programs, 30% of COBOL developers earning $100K+. COBOL has no community and almost no GitHub activity — but institutional oxygen tanks with infinite refill. It is not dying. It is becoming rarer and therefore more valuable.

Perl ranks #11 on TIOBE. On Stack Overflow activity, GitHub repos, and job demand, it falls to the bottom quartile. TIOBE is reading the fossil record of millions of legacy web pages. The language has a pulse on a chart — not in a codebase.

Haskell has near-zero job postings and 2% Stack Overflow usage. It also has 73,900 Reddit subscribers, an annual hackathon (ZuriHac), and a funded Foundation. It is the only language in our dataset that is simultaneously near-extinct in the market and culturally thriving.

JavaScript scores 2.0/100. The lowest in the index. The cockroach of programming. It runs in every browser on the planet. The only way it dies is if the internet dies.

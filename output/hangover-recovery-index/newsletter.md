# Newsletter — Global Hangover Recovery Index

**Subject line:** The world's best hangover country isn't Ireland

---

Here is something that will reorder your mental map of the world: Japan is the best country on Earth to have a hangover in. Not Ireland. Not Germany. Not Australia. Japan — a country that drinks less than 6.4 litres of pure alcohol per capita per year.

We just published the Global Hangover Recovery Index: 30 countries ranked not on how much they drink, but on how well they're equipped to survive having done so. Five dimensions, five years of public data, one genuinely surprising result.

**Japan (#1, 81.8 points)** wins because of infrastructure, not identity. The highest pharmacist density in our study. Liver-support drinks — Ukon no Chikara — in vending machines on every block. Seven-Eleven, FamilyMart, and Lawson staffed and stocked at 3 a.m. Japan didn't build this for hangovers. It built it for everything. Hangovers are simply a beneficiary.

**South Korea (#5, 76.8)** is the study's most striking story. It scores a perfect 100 on Cultural Cure — the only country in the dataset to max any single dimension. The haejangguk restaurants that exist specifically for the morning after. The convenience store hangover remedy product lines with clinical branding and genuine R&D. The South Korean hangover recovery market exceeds $100 million USD annually. When we say South Korea "turned recovery into a billion-dollar science," we mean it literally.

Meanwhile, the countries you'd expect to lead are nowhere near the top.

**Romania finishes 25th.** It drinks 17.1 litres of pure alcohol per capita per year — the most of any country in our study, nearly three times what Japan drinks. Its Hangover Recovery Score is 50.2. Low pharmacy density, restricted OTC access, minimal commercial cure infrastructure. High exposure. Low readiness. This is the Walking Wounded quadrant in one country.

**Ireland ranks 15th.** The pub-culture nation, the country that exported its drinking identity to the world, places behind Australia, Canada, and the Netherlands. Its healthcare access score (51.8) is the weakest of any country in the top half of the rankings.

**The United States ranks 18th** — entirely because of its Scheduling Slack score of 24.6, second-lowest in the study. Zero statutory paid vacation. No mandatory sick leave. The pharmacy access is excellent. The water is clean. The structural permission to stay in bed on Saturday is simply not there.

The full index covers 30 countries across five continents, scored on Healthcare Access (25%), Recovery Infrastructure (20%), Scheduling Slack (20%), Water Quality (20%), and Cultural Cure (15%). There's an interactive bar chart, a quadrant map plotting drinking against recovery, and a country-by-country breakdown you can sort and search.

The thesis, if you want one sentence to take away: the best hangover country isn't the one that drinks the most. It's the one that built the best morning-after infrastructure — whether it knew it was doing so or not.

## LinkedIn (2200 chars)

We ranked 30 countries on one question: if you had a big night out tonight, how well does your country equip you to recover?

The results destroy some comfortable assumptions.

**Japan ranks #1.** Not because it drinks the most — it drinks just 6.3 litres of pure alcohol per capita per year, well below the global average in our study. Japan tops the index because it has the world's highest pharmacist density, liver-support drinks in vending machines on every street corner, and 24-hour convenience stores stocked with recovery products around the clock.

**South Korea ranks #5 with a perfect Cultural Cure score of 100.** Its haejangguk restaurants are open through the night specifically for hangover recovery. Convenience store chains stock proprietary hangover remedy lines backed by genuine R&D. The South Korean hangover recovery market is worth over $100 million USD annually. That is not a folk tradition. That is an industry.

**Romania ranks 25th** — despite drinking 17.1 litres of pure alcohol per capita per year, more than any other country in our study. It has a genuine cure tradition (ciorbă de burtă, tripe soup) but almost no commercial infrastructure to make it available at scale. High exposure, low readiness.

**The United States ranks 18th.** It has excellent tap water (score: 96.2), good pharmacy OTC access (69.8), and a reasonable healthcare score (67.6). The single variable destroying its ranking: a Scheduling Slack score of 24.6 — second-lowest in the entire index. Zero statutory paid vacation. No mandatory sick leave. The country has nearly everything needed for a good recovery, except the structural permission to take one.

**Ireland — the country most associated with drinking culture in global imagination — finishes 15th.** Its healthcare access score of 51.8 is the weakest of any country in the top half.

The Global Hangover Recovery Index scores five dimensions: Healthcare Access (25%), Recovery Infrastructure (20%), Scheduling Slack (20%), Water Quality (20%), and Cultural Cure traditions (15%). Alcohol consumption is deliberately excluded from the scoring formula — it is a contextual variable. We are measuring readiness, not habit.

# Global Hangover Recovery Index
> The world's best hangover country isn't Ireland, Germany, or Australia — it's the place that turned recovery into a billion-dollar science.

## Opening

Every time a major drinking holiday arrives, the internet fills with lists of the world's heaviest-drinking nations. Romania. Latvia. Georgia. The implication is always the same: the countries that drink the most must also be the best at handling it. They've had practice. They know their way around a morning-after.

This index tests that assumption — and demolishes it.

The Global Hangover Recovery Index (HRS) scores 30 countries not on how much they drink, but on how well-equipped they are to survive having done so. Five equally-weighted dimensions determine readiness: the quality of your healthcare system, the density of pharmacies and OTC medicine access, how much scheduling slack you get (paid leave, sane working hours), the cleanliness of tap water for morning rehydration, and the depth of cultural cure traditions. The result is a portrait of national morning-after infrastructure — and the findings are disorienting in the best possible way.

Japan ranks #1 — not because it drinks the most, but because it has the world's highest pharmacist density, vending machines stocked with Ukon no Chikara liver-support drinks on every street corner, and 24-hour konbini staffed and ready at 3 a.m. South Korea sits at #5 with a perfect 100 on Cultural Cure — a category where it has built a literal billion-dollar hangover economy of recovery soups, convenience store cure lines, and specialized haejangguk restaurants open around the clock. Romania, meanwhile, drinks 17.1 litres of pure alcohol per capita annually — more than anyone else in this index — and finishes 25th. The gap between what Romanians pour and what their country provides to help them recover is among the starkest in the data.

## The Surprise

**South Korea out-scores Germany, Ireland, and Australia — despite being none of those countries.**

Western imagination places hangover culture firmly in Central Europe and the British Isles. Germany has beer halls and Katerfrühstück. Ireland has the full Irish breakfast and a national identity built in part around pub culture. Australia has… the pub culture that Ireland exported. South Korea, by contrast, barely registers in Western hangover discourse. Yet South Korea scores 76.8 — above Germany (76.3), well above Ireland (70.7), and comfortably ahead of Australia (72.8). The reason is a Cultural Cure score of 100 — perfect — that no other country comes close to. South Korea has dedicated hangover soup restaurants (haejangguk) that open at midnight and close the next afternoon. Its convenience store chains — CU, GS25, Emart24 — stock proprietary hangover recovery drink product lines with clinical-sounding names and real formula R&D behind them. The hangover cure market in South Korea is worth over $100 million USD annually. That is not a tradition. That is an industry.

**Romania ranks 25th despite drinking more than anyone else in this index.**

At 17.1 litres of pure alcohol per capita per year, Romania outdrinks every other country here by a comfortable margin. Its nearest competitor, Georgia at 15.5L, finishes three places behind it in the rankings. Yet Romania's Hangover Recovery Score is 50.2 — placing it 25th out of 30. Its Recovery Infrastructure score is 33.1 (limited pharmacist density, restricted OTC access). Its Cultural Cure score is 30.0. It has the ciorbă de burtă tradition — a tripe soup that genuinely works — but no commercial infrastructure to make it available at scale, no branded recovery drink aisle, no chain of 24-hour recovery-friendly food outlets. Romania drinks like a professional and recovers like a beginner.

**Ireland is aggressively mediocre.**

The country most associated with drinking culture in global imagination — whose export of pub culture has shaped bars from Boston to Sydney — finishes 15th. Its HRS score of 70.7 places it behind Norway (75.7), Denmark (73.7), Australia (72.8), and Canada (71.6). The culprit is a healthcare access score of 51.8 — the lowest of any country in the top 20 — reflecting the well-documented strains on Ireland's public health system. Ireland drinks meaningfully (10.5L), has a genuine cure tradition (full Irish breakfast, chicken fillet rolls, chip shops), but cannot convert strong cultural instincts into a top-tier recovery ranking without the healthcare infrastructure to match.

**The USA is throttled by zero statutory leave.**

The United States has the second-best tap water score in the dataset (96.2), a reasonable healthcare score (67.6), and genuinely good pharmacy OTC access (69.8 Recovery Infrastructure). On paper it should score in the 70s. It finishes 18th at 62.5, entirely because of its Scheduling Slack score: 24.6, second-lowest in the index. Zero statutory paid vacation days. Zero mandatory sick leave. No national entitlement to simply sleep it off. Every other developed nation surveyed builds in recovery time by policy. The United States prices it as a personal luxury.

You are in the most dangerous quadrant — high drinking, low recovery infrastructure. The cultural remedy traditions exist, but the commercial and healthcare scaffolding around them does not.

If you are from the **United States**, you have excellent pharmacies and clean water but the structural freedom to use them properly has been legislated away. Your 24.6 Scheduling Slack score is the story of American exceptionalism told from a hangover's perspective.

## Methodology

- **Index name**: Global Hangover Recovery Index (HRS)
- **Sample size**: 30 countries
- **Collection date**: March 2026
- **Scoring formula**:
  - `HRS = (0.25 × Healthcare Access Score) + (0.20 × Recovery Infrastructure Score) + (0.20 × Scheduling Slack Score) + (0.20 × Water Quality Score) + (0.15 × Cultural Cure Score)`
  - All sub-scores normalized to 0–100 before weighting
- **Data sources**:
  - Numbeo Healthcare Index 2024: https://www.numbeo.com/health-care/rankings_by_country.jsp?title=2024
  - EPI 2024 Sanitation & Drinking Water: https://epi.yale.edu/measure/2024/H2O
  - Alcohol consumption per capita: https://worldpopulationreview.com/country-rankings/alcohol-consumption-by-country
  - Pharmacists per 10,000 pop (WHO GHO / OECD Health at a Glance 2023)
  - Statutory minimum paid leave: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_minimum_annual_leave_by_country
  - Average annual hours worked: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_average_annual_labor_hours
  - OTC medicine availability: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7911380/
  - Cultural cure traditions: Atlas Obscura, National Geographic, Beverage Daily (qualitative rubric, 0–10 scale normalized to 0–100)
- **Notes**: Alcohol consumption is a contextual "exposure" variable used for the scatter chart only — it is not included in the HRS formula. The index measures readiness, not habit.

## LinkedIn (2200 chars)

We built a Survival IQ index for 30 horror movie characters. Here is what the data found.

The methodology: 6 metrics (Situational Awareness, Decision Quality, Weapon Proficiency, Group Leadership, Final Outcome, Panic Resistance), each scored 1–10 against film canon, averaged and scaled to 100. No supernatural adjustments. No genre exemptions. No mercy.

**The top finding:** Ripley from Alien/Aliens scores 93.3. This is expected. What is less expected is that the #2 spot goes to Erin from You're Next (90.0) — a character who showed up for what was supposed to be a weekend family visit, discovered her boyfriend's family had hired killers, and handled it like someone who had specifically trained for this scenario. She had not trained for this scenario. She just had the skills. The formula respects the grind.

**The surprise:** Ash Williams from Evil Dead scores 55.0 — mid-tier, #14 of 30. The conventional read on Ash is that he is a horror comedy punchline. The man reads the Necronomicon out loud. Twice. But his Weapon Proficiency is a 7 (chainsaw arm, boomstick) and his Panic Resistance is an 8. He adapts immediately and never stops problem-solving. The rubric defends him. His critics are wrong.

**The number that requires explanation:** Micah from Paranormal Activity scored 15.0. Georgie from IT — who is six years old, had zero information, and was chasing a paper boat in the rain — scored 16.7. Micah had a professional demonologist in his living room with specific, explicit instructions on what NOT to do. He bought a Ouija board. He provoked the entity. He filmed it. The index gave him a 1 in Decision Quality. That is the minimum score. He earned it.

**The special case:** Jack Torrance scored 28.3, and his low scores reflect moral failure, not cognitive failure. He didn't forget to lock a door. He chased his family through a hedge maze with an axe. The index does not distinguish between evil and stupid — it scores the behavior.

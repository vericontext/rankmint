# Horror Movie Survival IQ Index

## Overview
- **Slug**: `horror-movie-survival-iq-index`
- **Description**: 30 iconic horror movie characters ranked by how smart (or catastrophically dumb) their survival decisions actually are — scored across 6 clinical metrics, argued in the voice of someone who cannot believe what they just watched.
- **Items**: 30 characters from iconic horror films (1968–2022)
- **Layout**: Versus/Bracket (`templates/index_versus.html`) — new template

## Narrative Angle
- **Thesis**: The horror genre has a dirty secret: "final girl" status is not a measure of intelligence. Some characters survive by sheer genius. Others survive because the plot literally needed them to. And a distressing number of them die in ways that require zero supernatural explanation — only a basic failure of common sense.
- **Expected surprise**: Ash Williams is NOT the dumbest character in this dataset. He scores mid-tier. The man reads the Necronomicon out loud, yes — but he also immediately starts problem-solving, arms himself, and adapts. The real basement-dwellers are characters who had every warning, every exit, every resource — and still managed to die first. Micah (Paranormal Activity) is the spiritual bottom of this index: he was told, explicitly, by a professional, exactly what NOT to do. He made a list. He did all of it.
- **Hook**: "You don't need to outrun the killer. You need to outrun your friend who decided to investigate the noise. Here are 30 data points on who that friend is."

## Voice & Tone
All written copy uses roast/comedy mode — sarcastic, specific, zero hedging.

- Character intros: "Look, we need to talk about [character]."
- Low scorers: "Congratulations, you played yourself."
- Bracket matchup hooks: Hot-take framing — "This is less of a competition and more of a formality."
- Tier labels: Short, devastating, funny. No academic euphemisms.

## Scoring Formula
- **Formula**: Simple average of 6 metrics × 10 = final score out of 100
- **Each metric is scored 1–10**, then averaged: `SurvivalIQ = (SA + DQ + WP + GL + FO + PR) / 6 × 10`
- **Higher score = smarter survivor**

### Variables

| Metric | Abbr | Weight | Direction | Description |
|--------|------|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Situational Awareness | SA | 1/6 | Higher = better | How quickly and accurately they recognize that something dangerous is happening. Do they notice the signs? Do they trust their gut, or do they keep telling themselves it's probably nothing? |
| Decision Quality | DQ | 1/6 | Higher = better | Are their in-the-moment choices logical? This includes: do they go upstairs or toward the exit? Do they call for help? Do they split from the group when they shouldn't? |
| Weapon Proficiency | WP | 1/6 | Higher = better | Do they arm themselves? Do they use found objects effectively? Do they check if the killer is actually dead before turning their back? |
| Group Leadership | GL | 1/6 | Higher = better | Do they take charge in ways that help others, or do they freeze, panic, give orders that get people killed, or selfishly flee alone? |
| Final Outcome | FO | 1/6 | Higher = better | The cold hard result. 10 = survived AND saved others; 7–9 = survived; 4–6 = survived through luck or plot armor; 1–3 = died, with scoring penalty for how preventably. |
| Panic Resistance | PR | 1/6 | Higher = better | Do they freeze and scream into the void? Or do they convert fear into action within a reasonable amount of time? |

### Rubric: 4-Anchor Scoring Guide

**Situational Awareness (SA)**
- 9–10: Immediately clocks danger at the first sign. Does not need to be told twice. May even anticipate threats before they materialize. (Ripley, Laurie H40, Grace/Ready or Not)
- 6–8: Generally aware, notices most things, has 1–2 major lapses that are in-character and understandable. (Sidney, Adelaide, Erin)
- 3–5: Needs multiple incidents to accept reality. Classic "it's probably nothing" energy. May gaslight themselves. (Tree Gelbman early arcs, Dana/Cabin)
- 1–2: Functionally blind to danger that any reasonable adult would identify. Needs someone to be literally killed in front of them. (Micah, Georgie, Heather Donahue)

**Decision Quality (DQ)**
- 9–10: Consistently makes the most rational choice available given the information they have. Even their mistakes are defensible. (Ripley, Erin, Chris Washington)
- 6–8: Makes mostly good calls, with 1–2 genre-obligatory slip-ups that don't cause mass casualties. (Sidney, Grace, Nancy)
- 3–5: Makes about as many bad calls as good ones. The bad ones are memorable. (Ash, Tommy Jarvis, Marty)
- 1–2: Every decision is the wrong one. Actively creates new threats. (Micah, Rod Lane, Paxton, Tatum)

**Weapon Proficiency (WP)**
- 9–10: Arms themselves proactively, uses weapons with tactical competence, always double-taps, does not drop the gun. (Ripley, Erin, Laurie H40, Grace)
- 6–8: Uses weapons effectively when they have them, may fumble initially but learns fast. (Sidney, Adelaide, Nancy, Ash — chainsaw credits)
- 3–5: Gets their hands on weapons but uses them ineffectively or loses them immediately. (Tommy, Tree, Jack Torrance ironically)
- 1–2: Does not arm themselves when they easily could, or uses objects so poorly they'd have been better off running. (Micah, Barb, Heather, Georgie)

**Group Leadership (GL)**
- 9–10: Actively coordinates others, puts group survival above self-interest, gives useful instructions. (Ripley, Erin, Grace)
- 6–8: Competent individual survivor who attempts to help others with mixed results. (Sidney, Adelaide, Nancy, Laurie)
- 3–5: Largely self-focused, may help 1 person, may get 1 person killed. (Ash, Chris Washington — note: he's situationally isolated; score per context)
- 1–2: Actively endangers the group, splits the party, makes others' survival harder. (Micah, Tatum, Dennis/Scream, Rod Lane)

**Final Outcome (FO)**
- 10: Survived, killed the threat, saved others — complete victory. (Ripley/Aliens, Erin, Grace, Sidney)
- 7–9: Survived, threat neutralized, minimal additional deaths attributable to their decisions. (Laurie, Nancy, Adelaide)
- 4–6: Survived (or heavily implied) through luck, self-sacrifice of others, or plot necessity. (Tree — time loop, Ash — Chosen One status, Dana/Cabin)
- 1–3: Died. Score based on preventability: 3 = died heroically or through no fault of their own, 1 = died because they directly created the conditions for their death. (Micah = 1, Georgie = 3 — he's 6 years old)

**Panic Resistance (PR)**
- 9–10: Converts fear into fuel. Makes better decisions under pressure than most people do on a normal Tuesday. (Ripley, Erin, Grace)
- 6–8: Has genuine fear responses but routes them into action within 1–2 scenes. (Sidney, Adelaide, Laurie)
- 3–5: Extended panic phases but eventually stabilizes. Genre-appropriate freeze periods. (Nancy, Tree, Tommy)
- 1–2: Sustained, non-functional panic. Screams as primary coping mechanism. Freezes at the worst possible moment. (Heather, Tatum, Rod Lane, Georgie)

## Inversion Test
**Conventional wisdom top 3**: Ripley, Laurie Strode, Sidney Prescott.
**Inversion check**: Ripley will be #1 — that's correct and expected, and the roast voice makes it a weapon ("the bar is low enough that an alien-hunting warrant officer in a corporate space crew is the smartest person in this room, and these people are mostly Americans").

The real surprise is what the formula does to **Ash Williams**: his Decision Quality is low (3), but Weapon Proficiency is high (7 — chainsaw arm, boomstick), Panic Resistance is high (8 — he adapts fast and keeps moving), and he gets character-context credit for being a working-class guy with no training facing literal supernatural evil. He lands mid-tier. The formula defends him when the genre mocks him.

The confirmed bottom: **Micah (Paranormal Activity)** scores lower than the literal child (Georgie) because Georgie had zero information and zero agency. Micah had a professional demonologist tell him exactly what not to do and built his entire personality around doing it.

**Jack Torrance** gets an interesting special case note: he scores low on Decision Quality and Group Leadership (1, 1) but his Final Outcome is nuanced — he didn't die because he was stupid, he died because he was evil and then frozen. The formula captures this differently than "dumb character" framing, which is the counter-intuitive hook.

## Chart Strategy
- **Primary chart**: Versus/Bracket (new template) — 30-character single-elimination bracket. Seed by SurvivalIQ score. Matchups are annotated with hot-take one-liners.
- **Secondary chart**: Tier list (S/A/B/C/D) — the most shareable format. Each tier has a custom horror-comedy label.
- **Tertiary chart**: Radar — head-to-head comparison of selected pairs (e.g., Laurie vs. Sidney, Ash vs. Micah, Ripley vs. anyone). Shows all 6 metrics as a spider chart.

### Tier Labels (S/A/B/C/D)

| Tier | Score Range | Label | Description |
|------|-------------|-------|-------------|
| S | 80–100 | **The Professionals** | These people have clearly thought about this. Threat assessment, improvised weapons, tactical exits — they have a plan and they execute it. They would survive your horror movie. They would probably survive yours too. |
| A | 65–79 | **The Functional Adults** | Mostly competent, occasionally dumb in relatable ways. They make 1–2 decisions you'd roll your eyes at, but the math generally works out. You'd want them in your group. |
| B | 50–64 | **The Plot Armor Recipients** | They survive — but the formula is doing a lot of work. Half their victories are luck, circumstance, or the killer making a worse decision than they did. Let's not call it skill. |
| C | 35–49 | **The Walking Warning Labels** | They had enough information to survive. They chose not to use it. Functional people with a recurring software error in the threat-assessment module. |
| D | 0–34 | **Congratulations, You Played Yourself** | We need to talk about these people. Each one of them had at least one moment where the correct move was obvious and they did the opposite. The killer did not beat them. They beat themselves. |

### Bracket Quadrant Framing (Versus Template)
The bracket is organized into 4 seed-quadrant "regions" with thematic names:

| Region | Characters | Label |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| Top-left | Seeds 1–8 (S+A tier) | **The Survival Ward** — professionals who have done this before |
| Top-right | Seeds 9–16 (A+B tier) | **The Lucky Bastards** — they made it, barely, and we have questions |
| Bottom-left | Seeds 17–24 (B+C tier) | **The Horror Cliches** — you've seen them before, they haven't seen you |
| Bottom-right | Seeds 25–30 (C+D tier) | **The Darwin Lobby** — the gene pool thanks them for their service |

### Key Bracket Matchup Annotations (for the versus template)
1. **Ripley vs. Chris Washington** — "Alien-hunting warrant officer vs. man who figured out the sunken place and still couldn't leave the house. Different horror, same clarity."
2. **Laurie Strode vs. Sidney Prescott** — "Forty years of hypervigilance vs. girl who figured out the rules mid-movie. This one is actually close."
3. **Erin (You're Next) vs. Grace (Ready or Not)** — "Survival school dropout vs. woman in a wedding dress who just wanted a normal family. The upsets start here."
4. **Ash Williams vs. Tree Gelbman** — "Who dies more and learns less? Ash dies twice and shows up with a chainsaw arm. Tree dies 11 times and eventually extracts the information. Ash wins on raw efficiency."
5. **Micah vs. Georgie** — "A grown adult with professional advice vs. a six-year-old boy chasing a paper boat. The child scores higher. Let that marinate."

## Character Notes for Report Copy (Roast Voice)

- **Ripley**: "The curve-setter. The one person in this entire dataset who looked at an unknowable alien predator and said 'I have a plan for this.' She's not in S-tier because she was lucky. She's in S-tier because she earned it and then kept earning it across four movies and a TV show."
- **Erin (You're Next)**: "Look, she went to visit her boyfriend's family for a weekend, and it turned out his family hired killers, and she handled it like someone who had specifically trained for exactly this. She had not specifically trained for exactly this. She just... had the skills. The rest of you should be taking notes."
- **Ash Williams**: "Here's the thing about Ash. Yes, he reads the Necronomicon out loud. Yes, he does it again. Yes, in a later continuity he still has it in his trailer. But you know what Ash does the second the demons show up? He gets a chainsaw. He builds a boomstick. He adapts. His Decision Quality is a 4 because he keeps touching the book — but his Panic Resistance is an 8 because the man never stops trying. Mid-tier. We stand by it."
- **Jack Torrance**: "Jack is not dumb. Jack is evil. This is a crucial distinction that the index does not care about. He scores a 28 and he's wearing a tuxedo about it."
- **Micah**: "He was told. He was told by a professional. He was given a specific list of things that would make the demon angrier. He made a Ouija board. He provoked it. He filmed it. He posted it to whatever 2007's equivalent of posting things was. He got a 15. Congratulations, you played yourself."
- **Georgie**: "He is six years old. The index acknowledges this. The index does not adjust for it. He gets a 17. We are not proud of this. The data doesn't care."

- Include specific bracket matchup pairs: Laurie/Sidney, Ash/Tree, Grace/Erin

## Data Sources

This index uses qualitative scoring based on film canon. No external datasets required. All scores are applied by the data agent using the rubric and character guidance above.

| Metric | Source | Collection Method |
|--------|--------|-------------------|
| Character behavior canon | IMDb character pages, Fandom wikis, TV Tropes | WebFetch research + in-context judgment |
| Final Outcome facts | Film endings (survival/death) | WebSearch per character |
| Panic Resistance | Critical analysis (Horror Homeroom, WatchMojo, Collider) | WebSearch summaries |
| Decision Quality analysis | TV Tropes, WhatCulture, CBR analysis articles | WebSearch + WebFetch |
| Weapon use documentation | Fandom wikis per franchise | WebSearch |
| Group leadership behavior | Film canon + academic horror analysis | WebSearch |

# Horror Movie Survival IQ Index
> You don't need to outrun the killer. You need to outrun your friend who decided to investigate the noise. Here are 30 data points on who that friend is.

## Opening

The horror genre has a dirty secret. "Final girl" is a casting decision, not a competency metric. The monster doesn't pick victims based on intelligence — it picks based on screenplay requirements. But if you strip away the plot armor, the jump scares, and the "but she had to go back for her phone" scene, and you score these characters on actual, measurable survival behavior? The results are uncomfortable.

We ranked 30 iconic horror movie characters across 6 clinical metrics: Situational Awareness, Decision Quality, Weapon Proficiency, Group Leadership, Final Outcome, and Panic Resistance. Each metric scored 1–10. Final score is the average times 10. No supernatural adjustments. No genre exemptions. No mercy.

Here is the finding that should concern you: **Micah from Paranormal Activity scored a 15.0. Georgie from IT scored a 16.7. Georgie is six years old. Micah had a professional demonologist in his living room, handed him a specific list of things that would make the demon angrier, and Micah used that list as a to-do list.** The six-year-old, who had zero information, zero agency, and was chasing a paper boat in the rain, scored higher. Let that settle.

## The Surprise

**Ash Williams is not the dumbest character in this dataset. He's not even close.**

The conventional read on Ash is that he is a horror comedy punchline. The man who reads the book of the dead out loud. Twice. With enthusiasm. But the formula does not share this assessment. His Weapon Proficiency is a 7 (chainsaw arm, boomstick — the man improvised prosthetics weaponry decades before it was fashionable). His Panic Resistance is an 8 (he loses his hand and keeps going). He lands at 55.0 — safely inside Plot Armor Recipients, not basement tier. The rubric defends him. The critics are wrong.

**The bottom of the index is not who you think it is.**

Most people would nominate some combination of Tatum Riley (tried to crawl through a cat flap), Heather Donahue (narrated her own death), or Carol Anne (answered the TV). They are wrong. The confirmed bottom is Micah Sloat from Paranormal Activity. He scored a 15.0 — one point behind Georgie Denbrough, who is six years old and had no information and no weapons and no plan. Micah had a professional demonologist. He had specific instructions. He had a girlfriend who told him, repeatedly, exactly what was happening. He bought a Ouija board. He provoked the entity. He filmed it. The index gave him a 1 in Decision Quality. That is the minimum score. He earned it.

**Jack Torrance scores lower than Ash Williams on Decision Quality, but for completely different reasons.**

Jack's 28.3 comes from moral failure, not stupidity. His Situational Awareness is a 3 (he spent most of the movie rationalizing), his Decision Quality is a 2 (the axe through the door was not a moment of confusion), and his Group Leadership is a 1 (he was, definitionally, leading his group to their deaths). The comedy is that the formula captures all of this without caring whether it was evil or dumb. The formula just measures outcomes.

## Where Do You Rank?

You are not Ripley. Almost nobody is Ripley. But where do you fall on this spectrum?

**If you've ever said "it's probably nothing" out loud:** You are a Marty. B-tier. You'll survive, but not because of anything you did.

**If you've ever gone back inside to get your phone:** You are a Tatum. D-tier. The index is not angry. The index is just disappointed.

**If you've ever told someone "I have a bad feeling about this" and then gotten out of the car:** You are a Sidney Prescott. S-tier. This is the correct behavior.

**If you've ever thought "let's just check the basement real quick" and then actually checked the basement:** You are hovering somewhere between Dennis and Rod Lane. C-tier. The information was available. You chose not to use it.

**If you've ever said "we should split up":** Congratulations, you played yourself. You are the archetype. You scored a 15.0. So did Micah.

## Methodology

- **Index name**: Horror Movie Survival IQ Index
- **Data sources**: Film canon (primary); IMDb character pages, Fandom wikis, TV Tropes, Horror Homeroom, WhatCulture, CBR analysis articles, Collider — for behavioral documentation and character analysis
- **Collection date**: March 2026
- **Scoring formula**: Simple average of 6 metrics (each scored 1–10) multiplied by 10. `SurvivalIQ = ((SA + DQ + WP + GL + FO + PR) / 6) × 10`. Maximum possible score: 100.
- **Sample size**: 30 characters, including 3 composite archetypes ("Generic Blonde," "Let's Split Up Guy," "Runs Upstairs") representing recurrent horror film tropes
- **Scoring approach**: Qualitative scoring applied by rubric, using 4-anchor guidelines per metric. Character behavior scored across all canonical appearances. No supernatural difficulty adjustment. No age adjustment (Georgie's 16.7 is real and we feel bad about it).
- **Special case — Jack Torrance**: Scored as written. Low Decision Quality (2) and Group Leadership (1) reflect his behavior toward his family, which is disqualifying regardless of motive. Moral failure and cognitive failure produce the same outcome in a hedge maze.
- **Special case — Georgie**: He is six years old. The methodology does not adjust for this. The index notes it here and then posts the number anyway.

## LinkedIn

### Post 1 — Main Report Post

The most famous startup failures are not the most avoidable ones.

I ranked 30 famous startup deaths by Avoidable Death Score — a formula that weights founder delusion and product-market fit gap above raw capital destruction. Here's what surprised me.

**WeWork is #17.**

It raised $12.8 billion. Adam Neumann became a cultural villain. The documentary got Emmy nominations. And yet by ADS it ranks mid-table, between Brandless and Pets.com.

Reason: WeWork had $1.8 billion in real annual revenue. Co-working is a genuine, growing market. Its failure was a corporate governance disaster and a SoftBank valuation bubble — not a product that nobody wanted.

**Theranos is #4, not #1.**

Fifteen years of blood-test fraud. A perfect 10 on Founder Delusion. A perfect 10 on Ignored Warnings. And yet: blood diagnostics is a real, enormous market. The technology was fraudulent. The problem it claimed to solve was real. That distinction matters.

**The actual #1 is Juicero.**

$120 million raised. $699 Wi-Fi-connected juice press for pre-filled packets that you could squeeze with your hands. A Bloomberg reporter demonstrated this in 2017. The company shut down six months later. No user research, no willingness-to-pay test, no basic market sanity check could have missed this. The problem was invented by the company.

The formula: ADS = (Delusion × 30%) + (PMF Gap × 25%) + (Burn Recklessness × 20%) + (Ignored Warnings × 15%) + (Hype Multiplier × 10%)

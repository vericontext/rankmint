Quibi burned $1.75 billion in six months. Theranos sustained a 15-year fraud. Neither is the stupidest startup death of all time.

That distinction belongs to **Juicero**.

In 2017, a Bloomberg reporter squeezed a Juicero juice packet with their bare hands. The juice came out. The $699 Wi-Fi-connected Juicero press — the product that $120 million in venture capital had built — was unnecessary. The company shut down six months later.

Today we're publishing the **Startup Graveyard Index**: 30 famous startup failures ranked by Avoidable Death Score (ADS) — a formula that measures how preventable each failure was, not just how much money was lost.

---

### The Formula

**ADS = (Delusion × 30%) + (PMF Gap × 25%) + (Burn Recklessness × 20%) + (Ignored Warnings × 15%) + (Hype Multiplier × 10%)**

Founder delusion and product-market fit gap carry the most weight. Because burning enormous capital on a genuinely ambitious vision is tragic but understandable. Raising $41 million pre-launch for a photo-sharing app while Instagram existed — and calling it "the photo platform for the next century" — is something else entirely.

### The Three Biggest Surprises

**1. WeWork is #17.**

It's the most famous failure of the unicorn era. $47 billion peak valuation. Adam Neumann's excesses. Two documentaries. But WeWork had $1.8 billion in real annual revenue and operated in a genuine, growing market. The failure was governance, not product. By ADS, it's mid-table.

**2. Theranos is not #1.**

It scores a perfect 10 on Founder Delusion and Ignored Warnings. But blood diagnostics is a real, enormous market — the technology was fraudulent, not the problem. Juicero's problem was invented by the company. That's the difference between first and fourth.

**3. Vine scores the lowest (2.15) — and deserves to.**

Vine wasn't a startup failure. Twitter killed a working product with 200 million active users because of internal strategy confusion. TikTok then built a $100+ billion business on the exact same format. Vine didn't fail. Its parent company gave up on something good.

# The Startup Graveyard Index
> Quibi burned $1.75 billion in six months. Theranos ran a 15-year fraud. But neither is the stupidest startup death of all time.

## Opening

Quibi burned $1.75 billion in six months. Theranos defrauded investors for fifteen years. But neither is the stupidest startup death of all time — that honor belongs to a company that raised $120 million to solve a problem that didn't exist, built a $699 Wi-Fi-connected machine to solve it, and got exposed by a reporter who simply squeezed a bag of juice with their bare hands. Juicero is the #1 startup failure in the Startup Graveyard Index — not because it lost the most money, but because its failure was the most completely, inexcusably avoidable.

The Startup Graveyard Index ranks 30 famous startup failures by their **Avoidable Death Score (ADS)** — a composite metric that asks not "how much did they lose?" but "how obvious was it that they would lose?" The formula deliberately weights founder delusion and product-market fit gap over raw burn rate, so companies with massive ambition-driven losses — WeWork, Solyndra, Webvan — don't automatically win the stupidity trophy. Burning enormous capital on a genuinely ambitious vision is tragic. Burning $120 million because you never asked a single consumer whether they would pay $699 for a machine to squeeze a pre-squeezed bag is a different category of failure entirely.

The dataset spans the dot-com collapse through the 2020s unicorn era: 30 companies, five tiers, and one central finding — the size of the check is only loosely correlated with the magnitude of the stupidity. Some of the most inexcusable failures are mid-sized. Some of the largest losses are, by this metric, almost understandable.

## The Surprise

**WeWork is only #17.** It is perhaps the most famous startup failure of the 2010s — a $47 billion valuation that collapsed into bankruptcy filings and a documentary. It raised $12.8 billion. Adam Neumann's excesses generated years of press coverage. And yet by Avoidable Death Score it ranks mid-table, between Brandless and Pets.com. The reason: WeWork had a genuinely real business. Co-working space was a legitimate, growing market. The company generated $1.8 billion in annual revenue — more than most of the startups above it ever dreamed of. The failure was Adam Neumann's governance catastrophe and SoftBank's delusional valuation, not a product nobody wanted.

**Theranos is not #1, despite being the biggest fraud.** Theranos scores a perfect 10 on DelusionScore (physics-defying claims, 15-year suppression of internal doubts, courtroom denial) and a 10 on IgnoredWarnings. But it scores only a 5 on PMF_Gap — because blood diagnostics is a real, enormous market. The technology was fraudulent; the market was not. Juicero ranks above Theranos because its PMF_Gap is a 10: the problem it solved was invented by the company itself. No one needed a $699 Wi-Fi juice press, and a weekend of user testing would have revealed this.

**Cheap stupidity is a real phenomenon.** The quadrant chart reveals that several of the highest ADS scores belong to companies that raised relatively modest amounts: Color Labs ($41M, ADS 8.00), Lily Robotics ($34M, ADS 8.10), Clinkle ($30M, ADS 7.75). The "Cheap Disasters" quadrant in the matrix — low funding, high stupidity — is legitimately crowded. You don't need SoftBank backing to achieve world-class avoidability.

## Where Do You Rank?

If you have ever told a potential user "you'll get it once we launch" instead of showing them a prototype — you are in Juicero territory.

If you have ever said the words "we're not competing with X, we're creating a new category" while building something that is obviously competing with X — you are in Color Labs territory.

If you have ever raised a seed round based on a deck with no user interviews — you are in Clinkle territory.

If you have ever built a real product with real revenue and watched it get killed by corporate politics or a legal ruling you couldn't control — you are Vine. And that is a very different kind of loss.

## Methodology

- **Index name**: Startup Graveyard Index — Avoidable Death Score (ADS)
- **Formula**: ADS = (DelusionScore × 0.30) + (PMF_Gap × 0.25) + (BurnRecklessness × 0.20) + (IgnoredWarnings × 0.15) + (HypeMultiplier × 0.10)
- **Sample size**: 30 startups, dot-com era through 2020s unicorn collapses
- **Collection date**: March 2026
- **Scoring**: All five sub-metrics scored on a 1–10 rubric with defined anchors. Full rubric in plan.md.
- **Data sources**:
  - Funding and valuation: Crunchbase, Tracxn, CB Insights, TechCrunch funding round coverage
  - Shutdown dates and timelines: Crunchbase, Wikipedia, press archives
  - First red flag dates: TechCrunch, WSJ, Bloomberg archival reporting
  - Revenue figures: Annual reports, SEC filings, press estimates
  - DelusionScore, PMF_Gap, IgnoredWarnings: Qualitative scoring by RankMint using defined 4-anchor rubrics
- **Score weighting rationale**: Delusion (30%) and PMF_Gap (25%) are the primary drivers because they measure preventability. BurnRecklessness (20%) captures capital destruction relative to output. IgnoredWarnings (15%) rewards companies that pivoted quickly. HypeMultiplier (10%) adds context for valuation-to-reality disconnects.

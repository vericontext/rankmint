# Airport Anxiety Index
> The world's busiest airport is not the world's most stressful one. That distinction belongs somewhere far more unexpected.

## Opening

A traveler landing at Paris Charles de Gaulle for the first time faces a task that seasoned road warriors describe as genuinely disorienting: Terminal 2 is not a terminal. It is nine sub-terminals — 2A, 2B, 2C, 2D, 2E, 2F, 2G, 2K, and 2M — each requiring separate security re-entry, separated by bus transfers, and demanding an average walk of one kilometer just to reach a gate. CDG ranked sixth in the world by Skytrax in 2024. The data reveals something Skytrax does not.

The Airport Anxiety Index measures what lounge quality scores cannot: the lived stress of passing through a major hub. Thirty of the world's busiest airports were evaluated across seven operational dimensions — delay rates, security wait times, terminal congestion, wayfinding complexity, Wi-Fi quality, amenity quality (via Skytrax rank), and immigration queue times. The result is a composite 0–100 score where higher means more anxious. The findings challenge nearly every assumption that travelers carry about which airports are stressful and which are not.

The thesis holds: airport anxiety is decoupled from airport size. Atlanta processed 108 million passengers in 2024 — more than any airport on earth — and landed at rank 12, safely in the B-tier. Meanwhile, Sao Paulo Guarulhos (GRU), which handles a fraction of that volume, sits second on the anxiety ladder. Delhi leads the entire index at 81.4 points, driven by extreme gate congestion, a 25-minute average security wait, and Wi-Fi that consistently ranks among the worst of any major hub. The surprise is not that Delhi tops the list. The surprise is who sits just below it — and who is missing from the top 10 entirely.

## The Surprise

**Counter to expectations, CDG's anxiety rank is not driven by delays.** The narrative around CDG typically emphasizes its historic chaos and labor disputes. The data shows something more structural: CDG's delay rate (26%) and congestion (86.8/100 normalized) are genuinely high, but the airport's defining liability is its wayfinding score — a normalized 100.0, the worst in the dataset. Nine sub-terminals in Terminal 2, documented average walking distances of one kilometer, and confusing bus transfer requirements make CDG the structural failure case of European aviation. It ranks #6 at Skytrax. It ranks #10 in anxiety. The gap between prestige and practicality is widest at Charles de Gaulle.

**What the numbers show about Atlanta is the opposite of its reputation.** ATL's delay rate (73.9/100 normalized) is genuinely high — it's a busy southern hub with weather and traffic volume to match. But its wayfinding score (14.3/100) is among the lowest in the dataset, and its immigration score (12.5/100) is near the dataset floor. The underground Plane Train connecting seven concourses, color-coded concourse design, and Atlanta's role as a predominantly domestic hub with a simple transfer structure keeps it at rank 12. The world's busiest airport finishes behind Miami, JFK, Beijing, and Bangkok in anxiety terms.

**Tokyo Haneda (HND) is the index's most contradictory airport.** It records the lowest delay rate in the dataset (15%, normalized to 0.0), the easiest wayfinding score (2/10 rubric, normalized to 0.0), and among the lowest immigration queue times. Yet it has the highest congestion score in the entire dataset: 643,000 passengers per gate, normalized to 100.0. The reason is structural — Haneda's 140 gates handle approximately 90 million passengers annually, predominantly domestic, meaning each gate processes an extraordinary volume of short-haul, efficiently-moving travelers. The congestion score captures raw volume-to-gate ratio, but Haneda's domestic efficiency pipeline prevents that ratio from translating into the lived experience of overcrowding that the number implies. The airport finishes 25th in anxiety, in the C-tier — high enough to note, low enough to confirm it is functionally better than its gate-congestion number suggests.

**Mexico City punches well above its reputation.** MEX is routinely listed among airports travelers dread. Its wayfinding is genuinely complex (score: 71.4/100) due to the ongoing transition between the legacy NAICM terminal and the new Felipe Angeles airport outside the city. Yet Cirium's 2024 on-time data places it third globally — 84.04% OTP — and the data translates directly into a low delay normalization score (4.3/100). The result: MEX finishes 18th, ahead of Incheon, Sydney, and Frankfurt, airports that carry far better reputations.

## Where Do You Rank?

**If you fly through Delhi regularly**, the data confirms what your body already knows. The combination of 600,000 passengers per gate, 25-minute average security waits, and consistently poor Wi-Fi (35 Mbps estimated vs. SFO's 173 Mbps) produces an anxiety score of 81.4 — 11 points higher than the second-ranked airport. There is no equivalent in the dataset.

**If you connect through Paris CDG**, you are navigating the dataset's worst wayfinding environment. Budget time for unexpected sub-terminal changes and factor in that the famous CDG prestige — Skytrax rank 6, award-winning lounges — offers no relief at gate level.

**If ATL is your home airport**, the index validates the local instinct that it functions better than outsiders expect. Yes, delays happen (73.9/100 on that metric), but the architecture works. It ranks 12th, behind airports half its size.

**If you transit through Doha (DOH)**, you are moving through the calmest major hub on earth by this measure. A 12.3 anxiety score reflects the Hamad International model: a single organized terminal, approximately 2-minute average wayfinding, world-class Skytrax rank, and Qatar Airways hub efficiency. The numbers validate what frequent long-haul travelers have long reported.

**If your itinerary routes through Munich (MUC)**, the European comparison is instructive. MUC scores 16.2 — nearly one-third of CDG's 56.3 and one-fifth of Amsterdam's 55.1. Same continent, same era of travel infrastructure, radically different experiences.

## Methodology

- **Index name**: Airport Anxiety Index
- **Version**: 1.0
- **Data collection date**: March 2026 (reflecting 2024 operational data)
- **Sample**: 30 major global hub airports across 6 regions

**Scoring formula** (higher = more anxiety, range 0–100):

```
AnxietyScore = (delay_norm × 0.25)
             + (security_norm × 0.20)
             + (congestion_norm × 0.15)
             + (wayfinding_norm × 0.15)
             + (wifi_norm_inverted × 0.10)
             + (amenity_norm_inverted × 0.10)
             + (immigration_norm × 0.05)
```

All component scores normalized 0–100 within dataset range using min-max normalization. Wi-Fi and amenity scores are inverted (better quality = lower anxiety contribution).

**Data sources**:
- Delay Rate (US airports): US Bureau of Transportation Statistics / Squaremouth 2024 analysis — https://www.squaremouth.com/travel-advice/airport-travel-delays-in-2024
- Delay Rate (international): Cirium OTP 2024 — https://www.cirium.com/thoughtcloud/most-on-time-airlines-airports-2024-revealed-cirium/ and Eurocontrol Annual Report 2024 — https://www.eurocontrol.int/publication/all-causes-delays-air-transport-europe-annual-2024
- Security Wait Times: Upgraded Points spring 2024 study / Bounce.com — https://bounce.com/blog/airport-wait-times-2024
- Immigration Wait Times (US): US CBP Airport Wait Times — https://awt.cbp.gov/; iSelect Most Stressful Airports study — https://dailypassport.com/most-stressful-airports-new-study/
- Immigration Wait Times (Bangkok BKK): FlightQueue + traveler reports — https://flightqueue.com/airport/BKK
- Congestion (passengers per gate): Derived from ACI 2024 passenger figures divided by official gate counts (airport fact sheets, Wikipedia, aviation databases)
- Wayfinding Complexity: Scored 1–10 via rubric based on documented terminal structure, ATC complexity reports, FlyerTalk/TripAdvisor traveler reviews, and Travel and Tour World analysis — https://www.travelandtourworld.com/news/article/navigating-the-nightmare-why-jfk-paris-cdg-and-madrid-barajas-are-the-worst-airports-to-travel-through-here-is-what-you-need-to-know/
- Wi-Fi Speed: Ookla Speedtest Q1 2024 airport rankings — https://insidetowers.com/ranking-airport-wifi-and-mobile-performance-at-over-50-global-airports/; non-listed airports assigned regional median estimates from traveler reports
- Amenity Quality (Skytrax rank): Skytrax World Airport Awards 2024 — https://www.worldairportawards.com/worlds-top-100-airports-2024/
- AirHelp Score supplementary validation: https://www.airhelp.com/en-int/press/airhelp-unveils-the-best-and-worst-airports-in-2024-airhelp-score/

**Limitations**: International delay rates and security wait times outside the US rely on Eurocontrol averages, Cirium OTP summaries, and traveler report aggregates rather than direct airport-by-airport disclosure. Wi-Fi speeds for airports not listed in the Ookla top-50 study use regional median estimates. Congestion figures use estimated gate counts for several non-US airports where official data is not publicly available. All estimates are noted in `raw_data.csv` under `data_notes`.

## LinkedIn

**Long-form post:**

There is a word gamers use when a boss fight feels wrong: "unfair." And for the last decade, that word has been aimed almost exclusively at Malenia, Orphan of Kos, and Absolute Radiance — the hardest bosses in the hardest games.

The Boss Fight Fairness Index suggests this conversation has been misdirected the entire time.

I scored 30 iconic video game boss fights across six design metrics:
- How clearly attacks are telegraphed before they land
- Whether hitboxes match visible animations (no phantom hits)
- Whether the boss gives you real recovery windows
- Whether dying teaches you something you can act on next attempt
- How punishing the retry loop is
- Whether the camera and arena work against you

Difficulty was measured separately and kept out of the formula entirely.

The results are counterintuitive in the best way:

Malenia — the hardest boss in Elden Ring with a community difficulty score of 10/10 — scores 6.82 on fairness. Top 12 out of 30. She telegraphs her attacks. Her hitboxes are accurate post-patch. There's a checkpoint nearby. Her Waterfowl Dance is counter-intuitive on first encounter, but it's telegraphed. Her life-steal mechanic punishes recovery windows, but the windows are real. She's mostly honest.

Bed of Chaos, which most players clear on their second attempt (community difficulty: 4/10), scores 1.50. Dead last. Its branch platforms have hitboxes that don't match their visible geometry. The sweep clips through solid terrain. It demands platforming precision in an engine that wasn't designed for platforming. Every death to Bed of Chaos is the game's fault, not the player's.

The #1 fairest boss in the dataset is Baldur — the opening fight of God of War 2018. Community difficulty: 2/10. Fairness Score: 8.73. The easiest boss in the list is the most perfectly designed. Attacks are crystal clear. Recovery windows are generous. Retry is instant. The fight uses its narrative conceit (Kratos cannot be harmed by any weapon) to remove training wheels while keeping every design element honest.

The finding I keep coming back to: Isshin, the Sword Saint from Sekiro — arguably the hardest Sekiro boss — ranks #2 at 8.59. Each of his three phases introduces exactly one new mechanic. Nothing is hidden. The Mikiri counter window is the entire wind-up animation. You always know what killed you, and you always know what to do differently. That's what makes a boss fight hard in the right way.

Fairness is a design virtue. Difficulty is a tuning dial. They can coexist. The bosses that earn both — Isshin, Vergil, Sigrun, Gael, Margit — represent the highest form of boss design: experiences that demand everything from you while giving you every tool you need to succeed.

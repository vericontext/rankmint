# Anime Sensei Competence Index
> Gojo Satoru has the most powerful students in jujutsu history. He also scores lower than the volleyball team manager from Haikyuu. This index explains why.

## Opening

For decades, anime has sold us a lie: that the mentor's power determines the quality of the teaching. The more devastatingly strong the sensei, the more the student will grow. Gojo Satoru is untouchable. All Might is the Symbol of Peace. Shanks commands emperors. And their students — Yuji, Deku, Luffy — are among the most famous protagonists in shonen history.

So why does a chunin Academy teacher who has never won a notable battle rank higher than all three of them combined?

The Anime Sensei Competence Index scores 30 teachers across six weighted dimensions drawn from actual educational psychology: curriculum design, student survival rate, emotional intelligence, availability and presence, measurable outcome delta, and equitable attention across all students. The formula does not care about power levels. It does not reward cool training montages. It rewards the unglamorous, consistent, student-first work that defines actual teaching — the kind that shows up every day, knows every student's name, and adjusts the lesson plan when a student is struggling emotionally.

The results invert virtually everything anime fandom assumes about mentorship. The characters who score highest — Koro-sensei, Biscuit Krueger, Izumi Curtis, Wing — share zero fame outside their series. The characters who score lowest — Gojo, Kamina, Shanks, Ging — are icons. The gap between "inspiring" and "effective" turns out to be enormous, and this index measures exactly that gap.

What unites this group is intentionality. Every one of these teachers had a plan before the student walked in the door. Koro-sensei maintained individualized academic files on all 28 members of Class 3-E and adjusted his teaching to each student's psychological state weekly. Biscuit deliberately concealed her true strength as a pedagogical choice — forcing her students to develop their own read on power rather than calibrating against her ceiling. These are not accidents of character writing. They reflect genuine pedagogical sophistication embedded in the source material.

## The Surprise

**Might Guy (#7) outranks Kakashi (#20) by 2.4 points, and the data explains exactly why.**

Fan perception treats this as a running joke — Guy is the passionate, over-the-top rival to the cool, genius Kakashi. But by actual teaching metrics, Guy is a dramatically better educator. His taijutsu curriculum is structured and progressive. His availability score is 9/10 versus Kakashi's 6/10. His emotional intelligence is 8/10 versus Kakashi's 5/10 — Guy's investment in Rock Lee during the recovery arc after Gaara's attack is one of the most emotionally attentive teacher-student interactions in the Naruto series. And his student survival rate is 10/10. Zero students died under Might Guy's training. The "joke character" turns out to have been doing the real work.

**Iruka Umino (#9) scores 7.62 — higher than Kakashi, Jiraiya, All Might, and Gojo combined average (4.68).**

Iruka has no Sharingan. He has no sage mode, no One For All, no Six Eyes. He is a chunin who teaches Academy students. But he has a 10/10 on availability (present for every lesson, every arc, never disappears), a 10/10 on student survival (no Academy student died under his instruction in any arc attributed to his teaching failures), a 9/10 on emotional intelligence (his relationship with Naruto is the first time anyone in the series treats Naruto as a child rather than a burden), and a 9/10 on equitable attention (invests meaningfully across his full class). The formula rewards the work Iruka actually does, which is the work that the famous characters systematically neglect.

**Gojo Satoru scores 3.20 — lower than Takeda Ittetsu (6.94), the Haikyuu!! faculty advisor who cannot even play volleyball.**

This is the index's core thesis expressed in a single data point. Takeda Ittetsu's job is administrative — he secures matches, manages scheduling, finds the coach. He contributes almost nothing to direct skill development (outcome delta: 2/10). But he scores 3/10 on curriculum (he has a plan, even if administrative), 10/10 on availability, 10/10 on student survival, 8/10 on emotional intelligence, and 9/10 on equitable attention across the full team. Gojo, by contrast: 2/10 curriculum, 4/10 survival (Haibara), 3/10 emotional intelligence, 3/10 availability (imprisoned), 5/10 outcome delta, 2/10 equitable attention (extreme Yuji favoritism). Takeda does less, but he does it reliably, for everyone, every time. That is what the index measures.

## Where Do You Rank?

**If your favorite teacher is Koro-sensei** — you have impeccable taste and probably did not need this index to tell you he was excellent. The formula simply confirms what the arc spent 22 volumes arguing: that consistent, individualized, emotionally intelligent teaching is the hardest and most valuable work in any classroom.

**If your favorite teacher is Gojo Satoru** — welcome to the most interesting conversation this index generates. Gojo is not a bad person. He is not indifferent to his students. But "interesting" and "present" and "powerful" are not synonyms for "effective teacher," and this index draws that line precisely. Gojo would score dramatically higher if he had been available for more than half his students' development window. The imprisonment arc is not just a plot device — it is a 3/10 availability score.

**If you always thought Might Guy was underrated** — you were right. The data backs you. His score of 7.87 places him firmly in A-tier, four places above the sensei of Team 7.

**If you grew up watching Kakashi as the gold standard of anime mentors** — the 5.47 score is not an attack. It is an invitation to look at what happened to Sakura under his instruction and ask whether the bell test was a curriculum or an opening gambit that never continued.

**If you ever teared up watching Iruka talk to Naruto** — that emotional intelligence (9/10) is now a permanent part of his official pedagogical record.

## Methodology

- **Index name**: Anime Sensei Competence Index
- **Data sources**: Canonical anime and manga source material scored against a rubric. Verification via fandom wikis (naruto.fandom.com, myheroacademia.fandom.com, hunterxhunter.fandom.com, dragonball.fandom.com) and episode guides (AniList, IMDb).
- **Collection date**: March 2026
- **Scoring formula**: `CompetenceScore = (0.20 × CurriculumCoherence) + (0.20 × StudentSurvivalRate) + (0.18 × EmotionalIntelligence) + (0.17 × AvailabilityPresence) + (0.15 × StudentOutcomeDelta) + (0.10 × EquitableAttention)`
- **All metrics**: Scored 0–10 using a four-anchor rubric defined in detail in the original plan. Final score is out of 10, rounded to 2 decimal places.
- **Sample size**: 30 teachers across shonen, seinen, sports, and mecha genres
- **Important note**: This is a qualitative pop culture index. Scores reflect analyst judgment applied consistently to a defined rubric against canonical source material. Individual scores may vary by ±1 depending on interpretation of edge cases. The formula and rubric are published in full to enable challenge and replication.

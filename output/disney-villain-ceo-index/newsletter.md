## Newsletter Body (~500 words)

Picture the board meeting. Twelve seats. Fluorescent lighting. A Q3 deck that nobody wants to present. Who do you want running that room?

Not Maleficent, it turns out. Not Jafar. And absolutely not Gaston.

We spent the last month building a CEO scoring index for 30 Disney and Pixar villains — seven metrics, weighted by actual leadership research, applied to canonical film behavior only. Strategic Vision (25%), Resource Management (20%), Charisma (15%), Crisis Management (15%), Innovation (12%), Emotional Intelligence (8%), and a Ruthlessness Index adjusted for CEO-optimal levels (because a 10/10 sociopath is a PR crisis, not an asset).

The results? Deeply uncomfortable. Here are the three findings you need to know.

**Finding 1: Hades is #1 with 86.9 points, and it's not close.**

He runs an entire bureaucratic afterlife. He manages Pain, Panic, and a rotating cast of the recently deceased. He scores a perfect 10/10 on Resource Management. His Crisis Management (9/10) reflects a leader who takes a flaming skull on the chin and immediately pivots to Plan B. His raw ruthlessness score of 6/10 puts him in the CEO-optimal band — threatening when needed, not gratuitously destructive. He is, in short, the villain you'd most want running your company, and the one you'd most want to avoid meeting in a parking garage.

**Finding 2: Maleficent ranks #17. She cursed an infant over a party invitation.**

The Mistress of All Evil has been the face of Disney villainy since 1959. She scores 51.8 out of 100. This is "Needs Coaching" territory. Her Crisis Management (2/10) and Emotional Intelligence (2/10) are both bottom-of-the-barrel scores, earned by a single canonical moment: she was not invited to a christening and responded by placing a death curse on the newborn guest of honor. That is not a proportional response. A CEO who retaliates at infant-curse scale over a scheduling oversight would not survive the ensuing board meeting.

The Maleficent fans will riot. We are ready.

**Finding 3: Gaston is the most instructive data point in the dataset.**

He scores 10/10 on Charisma — the single highest metric score of any villain across any metric in the entire index. He scores 2/10 on Strategic Vision and 2/10 on Innovation. Overall: 39.0, #25 out of 30. He is the Populist Demagogue archetype in its purest form: extraordinary at rallying crowds, incapable of running an organization. No one reads the room like Gaston. No one in this dataset has fewer transferable skills.

Every fast-growing company has hired this person at some point. Usually to lead sales. Usually during a period they later describe as "a challenging chapter."

The default radar comparison is Hades vs. Maleficent, which we recommend as an excellent way to start an argument at a dinner party.

Prince John is last. Crisis Management: 1/10. He cries when stressed. He would not be given a budget in this economy.
